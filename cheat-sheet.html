School hands you boxes. Work hands you an outcome. Figuring out the steps is the job now — and accountability is not perfection, it is being predictable.

Organize the work

So future-you can find things and pick up where you stopped

Break it down before you start
Five to ten ugly lines of steps, written before you touch the work. Most procrastination is not knowing the first move.
Name files findably
project_what-it-is_v2, not image9990.png. Dates as 2026-10-02 so they sort. "I did it, I just can't find it" reads as not done.
Decide where it lives, once
One cloud folder per class. Inside it, keep what you were given separate from what you made. That split prevents the worst disaster.
Lead with what the link is
Never paste a bare URL. Say what it goes to, then link it. An unlabeled link gets skipped, and the work inside may as well not exist.

Organize the time

A calendar is where you decide in advance what gets to happen

Every due date, day one
Syllabus week, every deadline from every class, one sitting. This is how you find the November collision in August.
Book the work, not the deadline
Two or three named working blocks before the due date. Unbooked time goes to whoever asks for it first.
Work backwards from the date
Due the 20th? Draft the 15th, feedback the 17th, revise the 18th. One scary date becomes four manageable ones.
Protect one block a week
Two hours, same time, never moved, catch-up only. Everyone falls behind; few have somewhere to recover in.

Showing up

What replaces attendance as the measure

Be findable
Reply within one working day. No answer yet? Say so, and say when. Silence reads as "not doing it."
Ask on day one, not the night before
Day one it makes you look engaged. The same question at 10pm the night before makes you look like you just opened it.
Make a move before you ask
"I tried X, it did Y, I think the issue is Z — am I close?" beats "it doesn't work," which makes someone else do your thinking.
Own the miss out loud
Three sentences, before the deadline: what happened, what you're doing, when it lands. No story.
Bring the work, not the apology
Turn up with something on screen. A rough draft moves a project; an explanation of why there's no draft moves nothing.
Be able to say what you did
The ninety-second version: what it is, why those choices, what you'd change. Work you can't explain gets credited to someone who can.
