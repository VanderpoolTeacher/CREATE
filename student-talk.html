Part two

Organize the time.

A calendar is not a record of what happened to you. It is where you decide in advance what gets to happen.

Organize the time

Four moves

Every due date, day one
Syllabus week: every deadline from every class, one sitting.
So you find the November collision in August
Book the work, not the deadline
Two or three named working blocks before the due date.
Unbooked time goes to whoever asks first
Work backwards
Due the 20th? Draft the 15th, feedback the 17th, revise the 18th.
One scary date becomes four manageable ones
Protect one block
Two hours a week, same time, never moved. Catch-up only.

Part three

Showing up.

Attendance was the measure for twelve years. It is about to stop being the measure. Here is what replaces it.

Showing up · being reachable

Three moves

  • Be findable. Reply within one working day. No answer yet? Say that, and say when. Silence reads as "not doing it." People cannot tell stuck from gone.
  • Ask on day one, not the night before. The moment it is assigned, ask what finished looks like. Day one it makes you look engaged. At 10pm the night before, the same question makes you look like you just opened it.
  • Make a move before you ask. "I tried X, it did Y, I think the issue is Z — am I close?" "It doesn't work" makes someone else do your thinking.
Showing up · when it goes wrong

Three more

  • Own the miss out loud. Three sentences, before the deadline: what happened, what you are doing, when it lands. Everyone misses something. What people remember is whether they heard it from you or had to come looking.
  • Bring the work, not the apology. Turn up with something on screen — a draft, a sketch, a broken version. A rough draft moves a project. A polished explanation of why there is no draft moves nothing.
  • Be able to say what you did. The ninety-second version: what it is, why those choices, what you would change. Work you cannot explain gets credited to someone who can.
The whole thing in one line

Accountability is
being predictable.

Not perfect. Not never missing.

They know you will reply. They know you will flag it if it slips. They know that when you show up, there will be something to look at.

That is rarer than it sounds. Which is exactly why it is worth being.
